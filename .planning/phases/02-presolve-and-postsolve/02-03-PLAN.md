<objective>
The four structural families, and the path they open that has never existed
before: a model answered before the simplex runs.

Purpose: empty rows, empty columns, singleton rows and singleton columns are
the base cases the rest of presolve cascades into, and three of the four can
prove infeasibility outright. That is the first time this solver publishes a
verdict without building a basis, and D-12 — as corrected — asks exactly one
thing of it: the new path must be judged the same way every other path is.
`bench/run.c` already compares status, iterations, work units, the objective's
bits and the digest across two solves with the basis cleared between them. The
work here is confirming that check reaches the short-circuit, not rebuilding it.

The free column singleton is worth naming separately: a column that is otherwise
free and appears in exactly one row can be substituted out, taking its row with
it. That removes a row *and* a column for one reduction, which is a bigger
structural win than a plain singleton column and a bigger postsolve obligation.

Output: four families in `src/presolve.c`, the short-circuit in
`jm_dual_simplex`, four round-trip tests each shown to reject first, and
whatever `bench/run.c` needs — possibly nothing — to cover the new path.
</objective>

  <action>
    Add four tags to `jm_presolve_tag` — `JM_PS_EMPTY_ROW`, `JM_PS_EMPTY_COL`,
    `JM_PS_SINGLETON_ROW`, `JM_PS_SINGLETON_COL` — and one more,
    `JM_PS_FREE_COL_SINGLETON`, for the substitution case that takes a row with
    it. Each carries the original index it restores and the scalars its inverse
    needs. Increment the matching field of `jm_presolve_counts` as each fires.

    Empty row: a row no live column has an entry in. If zero lies outside
    `[row_lower_i, row_upper_i]` the model has no feasible point and the outcome
    is `JM_PRESOLVE_INFEASIBLE`. Otherwise the row is dropped; postsolve
    restores its activity as zero, its dual as zero, and its status as basic.

    Empty column: a column with no entries in any live row. Fix it at the finite
    bound that does not increase the objective — in minimize-canonical terms the
    lower bound when the cost is non-negative, the upper when it is
    non-positive. If the favourable side is infinite the model is unbounded and
    the outcome is `JM_PRESOLVE_UNBOUNDED`; this is a sound proof and needs no
    ray. Attempt no unboundedness detection from any other family: every other
    case is the simplex's ray-based verdict and is out of scope here.

    Singleton row: a row with exactly one live entry, `a_ij` on column `j`. The
    row folds into a bound on `x_j` — divide both row bounds by `a_ij`, flipping
    the interval when `a_ij` is negative — intersected with the column's
    existing bounds. If the intersection is empty by more than the tolerance
    `02-04` will name, the outcome is `JM_PRESOLVE_INFEASIBLE`. Until `02-04`
    lands that constant, this plan uses an exact comparison and states in a
    comment that it is exact on purpose and that the tolerance arrives with its
    sweep in `02-04` — do not borrow one of the solver's scaled-space constants
    or the checker's caller-supplied one as a placeholder. Neither lives in this space, presolve runs unscaled on the
    model as loaded (D-04), and a borrowed constant that works is the hardest
    kind to remove later. Postsolve restores the row's activity from the
    caller's original coefficients, and its dual by whichever value makes the
    full reduced cost of `x_j` satisfy the checker's rule for the status `x_j`
    came back with.

    Singleton column: a column with exactly one live entry. When the column is
    otherwise free — both bounds infinite — it is a free column singleton: it
    can be substituted out and its row eliminated with it, so both are removed
    on one reduction and one `JM_PS_FREE_COL_SINGLETON` record restores both.
    When the column is bounded, the plain singleton case applies. Distinguish
    the two in the source and in the counters; they are different structural
    wins and folding them together makes the D-13 counts unreadable.

    Every one of the five records is pushed as it fires and replayed strictly
    LIFO. There is no special case in the arena for the record that restores two
    things instead of one — it restores a row and a column when it pops, and the
    replay order is still the only order there is (D-07).

    Derive each family's dual and reduced-cost recovery against `sign_condition`
    as read, and write the derivation as a comment above the recovery, one
    sentence per family naming which condition it is satisfying. Primal recovery
    is arithmetic and falls out of the equation the reduction folded away; dual
    recovery is a *choice* among values all consistent with the reduced solve
    and not all consistent with the original problem's optimality conditions.
    That asymmetry is where the phase's correctness risk lives, and a recovery
    written by pattern-matching against the primal case is the specific defect
    the review plan exists to catch.
