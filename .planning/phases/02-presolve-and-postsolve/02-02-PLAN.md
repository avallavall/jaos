<objective>
Presolve bills the counter. A reduction that removes half a model and reports
zero work makes the solver look free, and the work unit is a public contract
(D16) — so this is a one-way door, and it is walked through deliberately, in
its own plan, with its own entry.

Purpose: D-14, and it is the exact shape of phase 1's D-10. Every historical
work figure changes meaning the moment presolve starts charging. That is worth
paying, because the alternative is a budget a caller sets that presolve spends
without telling them, and a work limit that stops the solve at a different point
depending on how much presolve happened to do — which is the mechanism by which
a superset bitmap turned from benign into answer-affecting inside phase 1.

Output: the billing, its entry in `docs/work-units.md`, and a pinned change
detector. No baseline is touched here. The rewrite that acknowledges the new
meaning is `02-07`'s own task, for the reason a baseline is never rewritten as a
side effect of running a gate.
</objective>

  <name>Task 1: What presolve charges, and the door that closes behind it</name>
  <reversibility rating="one-way">Every work figure in `DECISIONS.md`, in the three baselines and in every past record changes meaning once presolve charges the same counter, and the work unit is a public contract from 1.0 (D16).</reversibility>
  <decision>
    Which `JM_WORK_*` weights presolve charges, at which touch points, and
    whether the fixed-point loop's per-round overhead is billed at all.
  </decision>
  <context>
    D-14 is locked: presolve bills the same counter. What is not decided
    anywhere in the source is the *rate*, and the rate is the part that cannot
    be taken back. `jaos_set_work_limit` is a public contract, the four weights
    become contract at 1.0, and the three committed baselines and every work
    figure in `DECISIONS.md` are readings taken under the old meaning. Choosing
    a rate here rewrites what all of them say.

    The precedent is directly in the tree and it cuts both ways. Phase 1's
    01-02 refused a criterion that would have made the dense scan bill
    `s->nvar` because meeting it would have corrupted work units on every
    instance — and in the same plan it declined to bill the bitmap words at all,
    because a word is not a variable and a rate for them would be a number with
    no measurement on either side. The unbilled `nvar/64`-read floor that leaves
    is recorded in `docs/work-units.md` beside the unbilled `alpha` sweep. Both
    moves are available here.

    What presolve actually touches: each round scans every live column's entries
    and every live row's entries, which is a nonzero count; each reduction that
    fires removes a row or a column, which resembles an elimination; and the
    reduced model is built once by copying nonzeros. The question is which of
    those three the counter should be able to see.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="nonzero-only">
      <name>Charge `JM_WORK_NONZERO` per nonzero each round actually visits, and nothing else</name>
      <pros>One weight, already defined as "a nonzero touched in a solve", and presolve's cost genuinely is proportional to the nonzeros it walks. Nothing new is invented and nothing needs a sweep. It is the smallest claim that is still true.</pros>
      <cons>A round that visits many nonzeros and removes nothing costs the same as one that removes a hundred columns, so the counter cannot distinguish productive rounds from the fixed point's last, empty one.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="nonzero-plus-eliminated">
      <name>Charge `JM_WORK_NONZERO` per nonzero visited, plus `JM_WORK_ELIMINATED` per nonzero a firing reduction removes</name>
      <pros>Matches how the rest of the tree already charges: an elimination is charged by what it does, not by which routine runs it, which is why there is one weight for it and not two. A reduction genuinely removes nonzeros from the problem the simplex then factorizes, so the second charge is the same event the factorization is charged for.</pros>
      <cons>Two charges over the same nonzero on the round it is removed, which is defensible but is a claim about relative cost, and this project's rule is that every number needs a measurement on both sides.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="nonzero-plus-round-floor">
      <name>Charge `JM_WORK_NONZERO` per nonzero visited, plus a fixed per-round cost</name>
      <pros>The fixed-point loop has an O(rows + cols) floor per round independent of how much it changes, which is exactly the argument the two existing fixed costs rest on.</pros>
      <cons>Requires a new constant, and a new `JM_WORK_*` weight is a bigger contract change than a new charge at an existing weight. No measurement exists for it and inventing one is the failure this project's first rule exists to prevent.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>

  </read_first>
