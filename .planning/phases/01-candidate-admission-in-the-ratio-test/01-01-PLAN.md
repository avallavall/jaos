<objective>
Replace `dual_ratio_test`'s dense scan over every variable with a scan over a
persistent nonbasic bitmap, wired end to end — storage, maintenance at every
site that changes basis membership, the scan itself, and a debug-build
cross-check against the scan it replaces — and prove the replacement produces
the same candidates in the same array positions.

Purpose: `admit_candidate` runs once per variable of the pricing row and is
14.98% of instructions on `truss` under callgrind, against `ftran_prefix` at
6.68%. Nearly all of those calls are made to reject a basic variable. This is
the O(`nvar`) half of D61's 36.5% and the half neither D82 nor D84 touched.

Output: `s->nbmark` and four `jm_nonbasic_*` primitives in `src/simplex.c`,
declared in `src/jaos_internal.h`; maintenance at eight sites; the dense
branch rewritten; a `#ifndef NDEBUG` cross-check; and unit tests that were
shown to reject a broken maintenance sequence before being believed.

**The work-unit charge is not touched by this plan.** `simplex.c:1576` keeps
charging `s->nvar * JM_WORK_NONZERO`, so this plan's claim is the strongest
one available: digests *and* work units both unmoved. Changing the charge is
D-09 and lands in plan `01-02`, behind its own gate, because D16 makes the
work unit a public contract.
</objective>

<design_decisions>

CONTEXT.md leaves three things to Claude's discretion. They are settled here so
the executor implements rather than chooses.

**The representation is a persistent bitmap, `s->nbmark`.** One `uint64_t` per
64 variables, `(nvar + 63) / 64` words, bit `v` set exactly when
`status[v] != JM_BASIC`. Chosen over an explicit index list or a linked list
for four reasons, in order of weight:

1. Maintenance is one bit flip, which is O(1) with no amortisation argument
   needed — D-03 asks for O(1) amortised and this is O(1) outright.
2. Ascending order falls out of bit position. There is no insertion position to
   compute, so the stale-neighbour failure RESEARCH.md works through in
   Pitfall 1 cannot be written at all, rather than being written and then
   tested for.
3. The scan stays ascending and sequential over `s->alpha[]`. This is not a
   detail: the whole question D-11 measures is whether trading a dense
   sequential walk for a sparser one wins or loses on locality, and a
   representation that scattered the `alpha` reads would answer a different
   question.
4. It is the mechanism this codebase already built, shipped and measured —
   `amark` plus `__builtin_ctzll` in `jm_pattern_order` (`src/simplex.c:1637`).

**It deviates from the bitmap-clears-itself convention, deliberately.** `amark`
and `rmark` are zero between iterations and clear the words they set, which is
what lets them be allocated once. `nbmark` is the opposite: it is *persistent*,
it survives every iteration, and clearing it would destroy the thing it is for.
`jm_nonbasic_build` is the only routine that writes it wholesale. The header
comment must say this outright, because a reader arriving from `amark` will
otherwise assume the convention holds.

**The debug cross-check compares both scans**, as CONTEXT.md's discretion
clause requires — not the weaker "bitmap agrees with `status[]`" check. It runs
the old dense loop over the same solver state and compares the resulting
candidate arrays position for position.

**The tests live in `tests/test_simplex.c`**, in a new cluster after the
`test_pattern_order_*` block, because the primitives are that block's closest
sibling in both subject and shape.

</design_decisions>

<site_table>

## Every place basis membership changes — eight sites, not six

`01-RESEARCH.md` and `01-PATTERNS.md` both build this table from `s->status[v] = `
assignment forms and both report six membership-changing sites. **That table is
incomplete.** Two further sites change membership by `memcpy` over the whole
`status` array and carry no assignment form at all:

| Line | Function | What it does to membership | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720, 740-752 | `build_initial_basis` | every logical basic, every structural nonbasic | `jm_nonbasic_build` after the structural loop |
| 840, 851-857 | `build_warm_basis` | same, from the stored basis | `jm_nonbasic_build` after the loop |
| 1155-1163 | `repair_singular_basis` | evicts one, enters one, in a loop | `jm_nonbasic_insert(leaving)`, `jm_nonbasic_remove(entering)` |
| **2061, 2064** | **`pivot`** | **one leaves, one enters** | **`jm_nonbasic_insert(leaving)`, `jm_nonbasic_remove(q)`** |
| **2445** | **`take_best_if_better`** | **whole array restored from `bst_status`** | **`jm_nonbasic_build` after the `where` rebuild loop** |
| **2464** | **`restore_settled`** | **whole array restored from `sav_status`** | **`jm_nonbasic_build` after the `where` rebuild loop** |

The two restore sites are the dangerous ones precisely because they are absent
from the research: a `grep` for the assignment form finds them not at all, and a
bitmap left stale across a restore desynchronises silently — the solve continues,
the answer is merely *different*, and only a digest says so. Both already rebuild
`s->where` from `s->basis` in an explicit loop immediately after the `memcpy`
block; the bitmap rebuild belongs in exactly that place, for exactly the same
reason `where` is rebuilt there.

The three bound-flip sites — `apply_flips:1470`, `repair_dual_infeasibility:2164`
and `arm_reentry:2586` — toggle `JM_AT_LOWER` against `JM_AT_UPPER` and never
touch `JM_BASIC`. They need no hook. Adding one there is not harmless: it would
be maintenance keyed on bound status, which is the mistake that drops every
`JM_FREE` variable.

</site_table>
