  <action>
    Edit `DECISIONS.md` with scoped replacements. It is over six thousand lines,
    and its headings are cited from source comments and linked from its own
    index — a whole-file rewrite would break live references. Never renumber an
    existing entry.

    Add the index line at the top of the file, immediately after D92's, in the
    established form the neighbouring lines use, with the anchor derived from
    the heading exactly as they derive theirs. Add the entry itself at the end
    of the file, numbered one past D92.

    The heading is the decision, not the topic. It states what is now settled in
    the project's voice, so that reading the index gives the argument and
    opening the entry gives the numbers. A heading naming the subject area would
    be wrong; a sentence stating what the ratio test now does, with a second
    clause carrying the thing a reader would otherwise get wrong, is right.

    The body carries four things, and without all four it is not finished.

    **The question as it was actually asked, including what was expected.**
    `admit_candidate` ran once per variable of the pricing row and stood at
    14.98% of instructions on `truss` against `ftran_prefix` at 6.68% — the
    O(`nvar`) half of D61's 36.5%, and the half neither D82 nor D84 touched.
    The expectation was that skipping the basics, which are the large majority
    of the rejections, would show as a time saving. State that expectation
    plainly: an entry that does not say what was expected cannot say whether the
    result was a surprise.

    **The measurement, per instance, with the instances named.** The geometric
    mean of per-instance time ratios over the standard set at `J=1`, never a sum
    — two instances are 74% of that set's total (D46). `truss` on its own line.
    Every instance that moved against the mean named rather than averaged away.
    The callgrind reading beside it, with its own limits stated: instructions
    are not seconds and callgrind cannot see locality, which is exactly what
    this change trades. And the null result stated as a claim, because it is
    checkable and it is the strongest sentence available about a change designed
    to be invisible — **all 139 digests unmoved across all three instance
    sets**, plus the warm campaigns agreeing warm against cold with the
    independent checker refusing neither answer.

    Show the derivation of the 4.2% threshold rather than asserting it — three
    times the harness's measured repeatability — and reconcile the two
    repeatability figures the project carries: D-13 derives 4.2% from 1.4%,
    while the `jaos-measure` skill cites 1.3% from D60, which would give 3.9%.
    Say which figure the derivation used and say what the other one is. A
    threshold here is a number with a measurement on both sides of it, and two
    numbers in circulation for one quantity is what costs a week three years
    from now.

    Record the work-unit definition change explicitly. D16 makes the work unit a
    public contract, and this phase changed what the dense branch of the ratio
    test charges: from the total variable count to the count actually visited.
    Give the figures on both sides on at least two named instances, and state
    that dense-scan work figures recorded before this commit are not comparable
    with those after — they are not smaller, they are differently defined.

    **What was refuted, in enough detail that nobody re-tries it.** Two things
    at minimum, and they are the part of the entry that pays:

    - The membership invariant is that a variable is not basic, and never that
      it has a finite bound. A structure keyed on bound status silently drops
      every free variable, and free variables are created at three sites.
    - **The site table built by grepping for assignments to the status array is
      incomplete.** It finds six membership-changing sites and there are eight:
      `take_best_if_better` and `restore_settled` each restore the whole status
      array by `memcpy` and carry no assignment form at all. A membership
      structure left stale across either of them desynchronises silently — the
      solve continues, and the answer is merely different. Both rebuild
      `s->where` from `s->basis` immediately after the restore, and the
      membership rebuild belongs in exactly that place for exactly the same
      reason.

    Record what Harris's two-pass guarantee got, since the roadmap asks for it
    by name: the admission rule's body was not touched, the guarantee is a pure
    function of the candidate set and the order it arrives in, both are
    preserved by construction, and **the case the instrument had to refuse was
    built and confirmed refused** — a deliberately broken maintenance sequence
    in the unit tests, and an injected fault in `jm_nonbasic_remove` confirmed
    to abort a solve through the debug cross-check, both before the tests'
    passing was treated as evidence. Name the injected fault and name what
    caught it.

    Record that the decision preceded the code, and where. The source item said
    this needs its own decision before any code; that decision was taken and
    written down in
    `.planning/phases/01-candidate-admission-in-the-ratio-test/01-CONTEXT.md`
    on 2026-08-12, before any implementation commit, and D93 is that decision
    closed with the measurement rather than a decision taken after the fact.

    **What is left open, handed explicitly onward.** `PLAN.md` is archived since
    2026-08-12 and `.planning/ROADMAP.md` is where open work lives now, so hand
    it there. At minimum: restricting the candidate set ahead of `bfrt_walk` and
    `jm_harris_pick` remains available as its own decision and was not attempted
    here; widening the hyper-sparse path so the pricing row's pattern exists
    more often is Phase 3's `REQ-hyper-sparse-downstream-results`; and a
    trajectory sweep over `REFACTOR_EVERY` is raised by the roadmap's Open
    Question 5 and was not scheduled here, though this phase changed the pivot
    path.

    If the verdict was REJECT or INCONCLUSIVE, the entry is a refusal and takes
    D82 and D84's shape: what it does not pay, and what it cost to establish
    that. Say whether the code stayed in the tree or was reverted, and if it
    stayed, say what justifies keeping it on evidence that did not clear the
    threshold. A refusal entry is not a shorter entry — it carries the same four
    parts, and its refuted section is the whole point of it.
