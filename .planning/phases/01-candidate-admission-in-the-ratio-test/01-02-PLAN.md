## The one-way door, decided in advance rather than mid-flight

**Decision: proceed.** The dense branch's charge changes from `s->nvar` to the
count of variables actually visited (D-09). Authorised by the developer on
2026-08-12, before execution started, together with the consequence: the three
baselines are rewritten in plan `01-03` as a deliberate step with its own entry
(D-10), never as a side effect of running a gate.

This was emitted as a `checkpoint:decision` and has been converted to a
pre-authorised record. It is not a suppressed gate — the choice it asked for was
already made in `01-CONTEXT.md` as D-09, and the plan's own checkpoint text said
so: "CONTEXT.md already records the developer choosing D-09; this checkpoint is
not re-asking that choice." Re-asking it mid-run would have cost an executor
cold-start to confirm something already written down.

The reasoning is kept below because D93 has to account for it.

**`verify.plan-structure` warns about this plan, and the warning is correct.**
It reports that a task rated `one-way` has no preceding `checkpoint:decision`.
That is exactly what was done and it is deliberate: the door is still one-way,
the rating stays on the task because it is true, and the confirmation moved
from mid-run to this block. The warning is not to be silenced by deleting the
`<reversibility>` rating — the rating is the honest part. If you are reading
this plan cold and that warning worries you, this paragraph is the answer.

<why_it_is_one_way>
D16 makes the work unit a public contract. The moment this charge changes, every
historical work figure for the dense scan becomes a number from a *different
definition* — not a smaller number, a differently-defined one — and figures
taken before and after this commit are not comparable. Three committed baselines
(`bench/netlib.baseline`, `bench/netlib-kennington.baseline`,
`bench/netlib-infeas.baseline`) and the pinned constant in
`tests/test_simplex.c` all stop describing the current accounting on the same
commit.

**What is not affected:** solution digests, iteration counts, objective values
and every checker figure. If any of those move, that is a defect in plan
`01-01`, not a consequence of this decision — and plan `01-03` is what finds
out.

**The rejected alternative**, kept because a refusal needs its reason: keep
charging `s->nvar`, and close the phase on digests and seconds. Every historical
work figure would stay comparable and no baseline would need rewriting. It was
rejected because the counter would then report a cost for variables the solver
no longer looks at — the counter lying by exactly the amount this phase saved —
leaving the phase resting entirely on a WSL time ratio, and D17 says a WSL
number is a development number.
</why_it_is_one_way>
</pre_authorised_decision>
