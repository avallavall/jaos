# SPECS — everything JAOS must have

JAOS is a linear and mixed-integer programming solver written from scratch in
C23. No dependencies, Apache 2.0, Linux/GCC. This file is the whole target:
one row per feature, with where it stands. **done**, **partial** (says what
is missing), **missing**, or **out of scope**. A row changes status when its
feature lands; nothing else changes here.

Two rules hold on every commit. The answer is bit-identical on every machine
and every run. An independent checker, sharing no code with the solver,
judges every answer against the model as loaded.

`docs/feature-matrix.md` puts these rows beside HiGHS, SoPlex, Clp, SCIP,
Gurobi and Hexaly. An empty JAOS cell there is a row here that is not done.

| Convex quadratic (QP) | **partial** | a separable objective `½ Σ q_j x_j²` on top of the linear one, `q_j >= 0` when minimising and `<= 0` when maximising, refused otherwise by column: `jaos_set_col_quadratic` and `jaos_col_quadratic`, kept through copy, add and delete, counted by the statistics; MPS `QUADOBJ` and `QMATRIX` diagonals read and `QUADOBJ` written, the LP dialect's `[ q x ^ 2 ] / 2` block (or `x * x`, or the block undivided) read and written, off-diagonal entries refused by line; the barrier carries `q_j` in `Θ^{-1}` and in the residuals, presolve is skipped, the crossover is not run because a QP optimum is not a vertex, and the checker judges the KKT conditions with the gradient `c + Q x` and the dual objective less `½ x^T Q x`. The dual is the default algorithm, so a quadratic model simply solves, a MIP with one through the tree on barrier relaxations (the row below); `primal` and `pdlp` are refused by name. Python `x * x` and `x ** 2` in an objective at both layers. **A full `Q` since 2026-09-10**: `jaos_set_quadratic` takes it as triplets and the objective is `c'x + ½ x'Qx`, `jaos_quadratic_nz` and `jaos_quadratic` read it back as the lower triangle, and Python has `set_quadratic` and `quadratic` at the ctypes layer. Each off-diagonal pair is given once and a pair given twice is refused. The diagonal stays in `col_quad` and the strict lower triangle beside it, so `jaos_set_col_quadratic` still means what it meant. Every format carries it: MPS `QUADOBJ` names the lower triangle and `QMATRIX` both halves, one rule reading either and refusing two halves that disagree; the LP dialect's `q x * y`; QPLIB's lower triangle, written with the type letter `C`; and OSiL's `<qTerm>` off the diagonal. The barrier takes `Q` into the augmented system and switches to it on its own, because the normal equations cannot hold an off-diagonal `Q`. Convexity is no longer decided one column at a time: `σQ` plus a ridge of 1e-10 of its largest entry is factored by the quasi-definite LDL and every pivot has to come out positive, so a semi-definite `Q` is taken and an indefinite one refused by name. **What the published point costs**, measured 2026-09-10 over 3000 generated convex QPs (`Q = L L' + eps I`, so convex by construction): where the optimum sits at a bound, the answer is short of it by about 1e-4. `min -2x + x²/2` over `[-5, 2]` has its optimum at `x = 2`, the upper bound, and the barrier stops at 1.99984659 with a reduced cost of -1.5e-4 where an interior point wants zero, so the checker refuses the dual side; the objective is still right to 1e-8, because the function is flat there. The barrier converges on complementarity, and 1.5e-4 times 1.5e-4 is the 2e-8 it was asked for. About 2% of the generated set lands this way, and `jaos_set_col_quadratic` does the same, so this is the crossover's absence and not the full `Q`. **Two shapes hunted clean on 2026-09-10.** Maximised, convexity means `-Q` positive semi-definite, and the sign runs the other way through the convexity test, the barrier and the checker; nothing had read that path with a pair off the diagonal. Over 2400 generated models with `Q = -(L L' + eps I)` and the optimum known by construction, 9265 checks over four seeds and none wrong: the maximised model reaches the constructed point, the same model with `Q` and `c` negated and minimised reaches it too with the opposite objective, a positive diagonal entry is refused by column, and a pair widened until a dense `LDL'` of `-Q` loses a positive pivot is refused by the factorisation. The two calls that write `Q` were read against the matrix they are meant to hold over 12000 generated matrices, 80271 checks over four seeds and none wrong: the whole thing in one call, the pairs in one call and the diagonal one column at a time, the diagonal first and the whole thing replacing it, one diagonal entry changed after the fact, a copy, a random subset of columns deleted, and a column added. Missing: a reading on a published QP set, and a push that puts a column the complementarity says is at a bound onto it, which is the crossover a QP can have |

| | status | |
|---|---|---|
| Dual simplex | **done** | steepest-edge pricing, Harris two-pass ratio test with bound flipping, phase 1 by artificial bounds, a cost perturbation on the first stall and Bland's rule after |
| Primal simplex | **partial** | steepest-edge pricing (Devex behind `cfg.primal_devex`, Dantzig behind `cfg.primal_dantzig`), composite phase 1, Harris ratio test; `jaos_set_algorithm` selects it. Missing: 5 of the 94 standard instances run past 10x the dual's work (`bench/results/primal.txt`): d6cube, dfl001, fit1d, fit2d, seba; none disagrees |
| Barrier (interior point) | **partial** | Mehrotra's predictor-corrector on the primal-dual pair with bounds (`src/barrier.c`), from Mehrotra's starting point with both of its shifts floored at `BARRIER_START_MIN` so a model whose cost lies in the range of `A^T` does not start at a complementarity of 1e-20, the normal equations factored by the deterministic sparse Cholesky (`src/chol.c`: minimum-degree ordering with ties by index, symbolic once, numeric per iteration, a tiny pivot replaced by `CHOL_PIVOT_HUGE`), primal regularisation scaled by the infeasibility and a diagonal on the normal matrix, every pass billed. `jaos_set_algorithm(JAOS_ALGORITHM_BARRIER)`, `--algorithm barrier`, `algorithm=barrier`, Python at both layers; plain LPs only, a MIP's relaxations stay on the dual. With the crossover below, `bench/results/barrier.txt` reads 73 of the 94 accepted by the checker at 1e-6, 0 failed, 21 past 10x the dual's work, work geometric mean 2.705x the dual; the barrier alone (commit 090e5ab) read 83 agreeing at 1.588x with 32 accepted. Infeasible and unbounded models get their verdict and certificate from the dual simplex, which takes over from the slack basis when the barrier's iterate grows past `BARRIER_DIVERGE` times the data, turns non-finite, or reaches `BARRIER_MAX_ITER` without converging (`bench/results/barrier-infeas.txt`: 23 of the 29 refused instances agree with the dual inside 10x its work, the other 6 hit that limit before the hand-off, none disagrees; the norm pass that watches the iterate is billed, work geometric mean on the standard set 2.689x to 2.724x). A column with more than `BARRIER_DENSE_FACTOR` times the average count is left out of the normal matrix and reintroduced by a Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury correction on every solve, falling back to the full form when the small system is not positive definite (fit2p from past 10x the dual's work to 1.64x, and cplex1 on the infeasible set the same way, `bench/measurements/02-223/`). The Newton step can also be taken on the augmented system, `[[-(Q + Theta^-1), E^T], [E, delta I]]`, factored by the quasi-definite LDL of `src/chol.c` with the sign of every pivot fixed in advance (`cfg.barrier_augmented`, off). It answers the same question, since eliminating the step in the variables turns it into the normal equations, and it keeps its shape when `Q` stops being diagonal, which is why it is there. **Measured 2026-09-10 over all three sets**, against the normal equations and at the same 10x limit: the standard 94 read 87 agreeing with 7 overruns against 73 with 21, work geometric mean 2.722x against 2.705x, `pilot87` 37399 iterations against 83384 and `fit2p` 0.286x, the best of the set, from a dense column the augmented form takes without the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury correction; the 29 infeasible read 25 against 23 with 4 overruns against 6 at 1.769x against 1.783x; and the 16 Kennington read 9 against 11 with 7 overruns against 5 at 3.078x against 2.309x, because those are large and sparse and `A Theta A^T` never densifies there, so the bigger matrix only costs. 123 instances better and the 16 largest worse is a trade and not a win, so the default does not move. What would move it is a rule that picks the augmented system where it wins without giving up Kennington. Missing: a crossover cheaper than a crash (the row below) |
| Crossover from an interior point | **partial** | from the barrier's point, every column and row is ranked by its distance inside its bounds against its dual slack, the best `rows` of them are the basis guess, the LU repairs the guess where it is singular by swapping in logicals of uncovered rows, and the dual simplex finishes from it under the existing warm start (`crash_basis` in `src/barrier.c`). Every barrier answer is a vertex with a basis. Missing: a primal and dual push; on the degenerate instances the simplex from the guess costs more than a cold dual solve (`d2q06c` 237232 iterations against 27935, `pilot87` 83342 against 37362), which is what the 22 overruns are |
| First-order method (PDLP) | **partial** | primal-dual hybrid gradient on the scaled model with the row bounds as slack columns, after `PDLP_RUIZ_ROUNDS` of Ruiz equilibration and the Pock-Chambolle scaling (`src/pdlp.c`): the adaptive step, restarts to the step-weighted running average on the KKT error and the primal-weight rebalancing of Applegate et al. (2021), single-threaded, deterministic (square roots only, no `exp`/`log`/`pow`), every matrix pass billed; stops at a relative KKT error of `PDLP_TOL`, finishes through the barrier's crossover, hands an infeasible, unbounded or stalled model to the dual simplex the same way. `jaos_set_algorithm(JAOS_ALGORITHM_PDLP)`, `--algorithm pdlp`, `algorithm=pdlp`, Python at both layers; plain LPs only. `bench/results/pdlp.txt` reads 22 of the 94 inside 10x the dual's work (fit2p at 0.45x, the rest 1x to 10x), 72 past it, 0 disagreeing; without the preconditioning it read 3 (`bench/measurements/02-222/`). It converges given room (afiro 262 iterations, adlittle 1041, sc50a 450, israel 1857, share2b 78773, `bench/measurements/02-221/` and `02-222/`) at 10x to 1000x the dual's work on the small instances, which is what a first-order method costs on models a factorization handles. The difference of successive iterates is read as a ray every `PDLP_CHECK_EVERY` from `PDLP_INFEAS_FROM` on, so a refused model stops before `PDLP_MAX_ITER` instead of walking to it: `cplex1` from 200000 iterations to 5292, `forest6` to 67853, `bgprtr` to 176345, each run alone. The ray does not say which of infeasible and unbounded it is, because a model can be both, so the dual simplex still gives the verdict and the certificate. It costs 0.10% of the work on the standard 94 and changes no iteration count there. `bench/results/pdlp-infeas.txt` is the reading beside `barrier-infeas`, and it cannot show this: it caps the method at 10x the dual's work, which is 314 iterations on `bgprtr`, so the ray never converges under it. Missing: a reading where it pays, which is a set too large to factor; feasibility polishing |
| Concurrent solve, deterministic | **partial** | `jaos_set_algorithm(JAOS_ALGORITHM_CONCURRENT)`, `--algorithm concurrent`, `algorithm=concurrent`, Python at both layers (`src/concurrent.c`): the model is copied three times, the dual set on the first, the primal on the second and the barrier on the third, and the three run in that order under a work budget of `CONCURRENT_SLICE` that multiplies by `CONCURRENT_GROWTH` each round. The first to answer wins and its status, values, duals, basis and certificate become the model's; the work billed is the sum over the three, so a model the dual settles inside the first budget costs exactly what the dual costs and the other two never start. Nothing reads a clock, so the winner and the work are the same on every machine and every run. Plain LPs only: a MIP runs its relaxations on the dual as it does under `barrier`, and a quadratic objective is refused by name as `primal` and `pdlp` refuse it. `bench/results/concurrent.txt` reads 94 of 94 agreeing inside 10x the dual's work, 0 failed and 0 disagreeing, work geometric mean 1.090x the dual and iterations 0.902x; the primal wins `grow22` at 0.372x, where the dual stalls 14176 iterations against its 610, and the worst is `stocfor3` at 6.75x. `jaos_set_threads` above 1 starts the three at once instead of one after another and stops every arm behind one that has answered, since an arm behind the winner is never the one published; the first round stays sequential, because a model that ends there is one the dual settled in milliseconds and the thread pays for itself only past that. Checked over the whole standard 94: the work units and the objective are identical at 1 and at 3 threads on every one of them, and the wall clock over the set falls from 132.6 s to 110.4 s, geometric mean 1.002x per instance and 1.102x over the 8 that take a second or more, `pilot` 1.78x and `d2q06c` 1.51x. Missing: a set where the barrier is the arm that wins |

Python at both layers. **A row that cuts nothing off, which is what a valid cut looks like from the tree's side, was two defects until 2026-09-10.** Such a row still goes on the node LP, and adding a row drops the answer that LP carries; the re-solve ran only where the point broke the row, so the tree read vectors it had just freed and the solve ended in a segmentation fault on a model as small as `min -x0 - x1` over `x0 + x1 <= 1` with two binaries and a row `x0 + x1 <= 2`. With the answer restored the re-solve can land on another vertex of the same optimal face, and a tree still holding its own copy of the point then took a fractional one under a verdict of integral and rounded it: on five integer columns it published -16 at (2, 1, 0, 1, 1), where the second row reads 3 against a bound of 1 and enumeration says -10. Both are fixed, the answer restored where the rows go in and the point read back where it is held across that. **Checked 2026-09-10 against full enumeration** over 4800 generated models of 2 to 6 integer columns and 1 to 3 rows, four seeds, each solved four ways: a model row written again, the same row with its bound raised, the column sum against the sum of the column bounds, and a callback that adds nothing as the control. 19200 solves, 20124 nodes saw the callback and 14620 rows went in, and none disagrees. The four gates and the barrier readings are byte-identical, because a node with no rows to flush pays nothing (`tests/test_mip.c`, `test_a_node_row_that_cuts_nothing_off_leaves_the_answer`) |

The bound intervals are read by the linearity alone, three solves with the parameter at each end and at the midpoint, which needs no sign convention for the dual and so cannot mistake one for a defect (`tests/test_ranging.c`, `test_a_degenerate_optimum_ranges_like_any_other`). A quadratic objective is refused by name since 2026-09-10: ranging is about a linear cost, a QP optimum is not a vertex, so there is no basis to hold, and the three calls used to report the basis count instead of the cause. A MIP is refused the same day and for a harder reason: the basis behind a MIP answer belongs to the last node of the tree, branching bounds and all, so the interval is about that node's linear program. It came out plausible and it was false. On `max 5x + 4y` over `6x + 4y <= 24` and `x + 2y <= 6` with both columns integer, the answer is `x = 4, y = 0` and the cost of `x` was given the interval `[-inf, 6]`; at a cost of 4 the optimum is `x = 3, y = 1`. SOS sets and semi-continuous columns go with it |
